# Options Basics

Options are derivative contracts giving the holder the right—but not the obligation—to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified price before or at a set date.

Understanding the relationship between spot price, strike price, time to expiration, and volatility is essential for trading effectively on Zenith.

---

<h2 id="core-concepts">Core Concepts: Calls & Puts</h2>

There are two primary flavors of options contracts:

### 1. Call Options (Right to Buy)
* A **Call** gives the buyer the right to purchase the underlying asset at the **Strike Price ($K$)**.
* **Buyer Perspective:** Profitable when the spot price ($S$) rises above the strike price plus the premium paid. Maximum loss is capped at the premium paid.
* **Writer Perspective:** Obligated to sell the underlying asset at the strike price if exercised. Writer collects upfront premium in exchange for upside cap.

### 2. Put Options (Right to Sell)
* A **Put** gives the buyer the right to sell the underlying asset at the **Strike Price ($K$)**.
* **Buyer Perspective:** Profitable when the spot price falls below the strike price minus the premium paid. Provides downside hedging.
* **Writer Perspective:** Obligated to purchase the underlying asset at the strike price if exercised. Writer collects upfront premium.

---

<h2 id="option-buyers-vs-writers">Buyers vs. Writers</h2>

In every options trade on Zenith, two parties enter an immutable contract:

| Role | Right or Obligation | Risk Profile | Collateral Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Buyer (Long)** | Has the **right** to exercise if option finishes in-the-money. | Capped at initial premium paid; profit potential can be unlimited (calls). | Zero collateral (pays cash premium up front). |
| **Writer (Short)** | Has the **obligation** to settle payout if buyer exercises. | Limited to premium received; downside risk if market moves against position. | 100% spot token for calls; 110% cash strike value for puts. |

<h2 id="moneyness">Moneyness: ITM, ATM, OTM</h2>

Moneyness describes the relationship between the current spot price ($S$) of the underlying asset and the strike price ($K$) of the option contract:

### In-the-Money (ITM)
* **Call:** Spot price is higher than strike price ($S > K$). The option has positive intrinsic value: $\max(0, S - K)$.
* **Put:** Spot price is lower than strike price ($S < K$). Intrinsic value: $\max(0, K - S)$.

### At-the-Money (ATM)
* The spot price is equal or virtually identical to the strike price ($S \approx K$). The option value is driven purely by time value and volatility.

### Out-of-the-Money (OTM)
* **Call:** Spot price is below the strike price ($S < K$). Intrinsic value is zero.
* **Put:** Spot price is above the strike price ($S > K$). Intrinsic value is zero.

> **Tip:** On Zenith's options chain, ITM rows are visually highlighted with muted emerald (`--call-dim`) for calls and muted rose (`--put-dim`) for puts, while ATM strikes are framed with amber badges.

<h2 id="european-style">European Exercise on Zenith</h2>

Options can be characterized by their exercise timing rules:
* **American Options:** May be exercised by the holder at any time prior to and including expiration day.
* **European Options:** May only be exercised at the precise expiration time.

Zenith strictly implements **European-style options**. This provides key advantages for decentralized finance:
1. **Predictable Collateral Lockups:** Writers know with mathematical certainty that their collateral cannot be called away before expiration day, avoiding sudden margin calls.
2. **Deterministic Settlement:** Exercise payouts are evaluated against the oracle price at the exact moment of expiration, eliminating front-running and early exercise arbitrage.
3. **Optimized Gas Efficiency:** Smart contracts only perform settlement calculations during the 24-hour expiration settlement window.
