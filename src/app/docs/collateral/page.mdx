# Collateral Rules

To guarantee solvency without liquidators or margin calls, Zenith enforces strict, mathematically deterministic collateral requirements for option writers.

When writing an option, collateral is locked directly inside the Soroban contract escrow until either the position is closed or the contract expires.

---

<h2 id="why-collateral">Why Collateral Matters</h2>

Traditional centralized options exchanges rely on portfolio margin models and automated liquidation engines. When market volatility spikes, liquidation cascades can cause severe slippage, unhedged socialized losses, or insolvency.

Zenith eliminates liquidation cascades by adopting a **fully collateralized escrow architecture**:
1. **Zero Liquidation Risk:** Option writers cannot be liquidated mid-contract regardless of how volatile the market becomes.
2. **Guaranteed Payouts:** Every open option contract has 100% of potential payout capital pre-funded in escrow. Buyers are guaranteed complete settlement.
3. **No Socialized Losses:** Insolvency of one participant cannot impact other participants in the protocol.

---

<h2 id="covered-calls">Covered Calls (100% Spot)</h2>

When writing a **Call Option**, the writer commits to delivering the underlying asset at strike price $K$ if exercised.

To guarantee delivery, the writer must deposit **100% of the underlying asset** into the Soroban contract:

<BlockMath math="\text{Collateral}_{\text{call}} = N \cdot 1.00 \; \text{units of Underlying Asset}" />

where $N$ is the number of contracts written.

* **Example:** Writing 1,000 XLM Call options requires locking exactly **1,000 XLM** in the contract.
* **USD Equivalence:** In fiat terms, the equivalent spot value locked is $N \cdot S$.
* **Outcome on Expiry:**
  * If $S \le K$ (OTM): The option expires worthless. The writer reclaims 100% of their locked 1,000 XLM, retaining the buyer's premium.
  * If $S > K$ (ITM): The contract pays the intrinsic difference $(S - K) \cdot N$ to the buyer, and returns any remaining balance to the writer.

---

<h2 id="cash-secured-puts">Cash-Secured Puts (110% Over-Collateralization)</h2>

When writing a **Put Option**, the writer commits to buying the underlying asset at strike price $K$ if the buyer exercises.

To ensure total solvency and provide a robust safety buffer against sudden downward price gaps prior to expiration, Zenith requires **110% cash over-collateralization** locked in USDC:

<BlockMath math="\text{Collateral}_{\text{put}} = N \cdot K \cdot 1.10 \; \text{USDC}" />

* **Base Strike Obligation ($100\%$):** $N \cdot K$ represents the total potential cash needed if the asset's price drops to zero.
* **Safety Buffer ($10\%$):** An additional $10\%$ buffer ($N \cdot K \cdot 0.10$) protects the protocol against slippage or extreme oracle deviations during expiration settlement.
* **Example:** Writing 1,000 puts on XLM at strike $K = \$0.12$:
  * Base obligation: $1,000 \times \$0.12 = \$120.00$ USDC
  * 10% safety buffer: $\$12.00$ USDC
  * Total collateral locked: **$132.00 USDC**

<h2 id="lock-and-release">Locking & Release Lifecycle</h2>

Collateral moves through four transparent phases during an option's lifetime:

```text
[Position Open] ──► [Collateral Escrowed] ──► [Expiry / Close] ──► [Payout & Release]
   Writer approves     Locked in Soroban       Position closes       Buyer receives ITM
   SEP-41 allowance    smart contract          or 24h window hits    Writer receives rest
```

1. **Authorization:** Writer approves the Zenith Soroban contract to transfer the required tokens via standard SEP-41 token allowance.
2. **Escrow:** Upon writing, tokens are transferred into contract storage. The writer cannot withdraw these funds until the position terminates.
3. **Closing Position:** The writer can buy back the position on the open market at current market price, releasing the locked collateral immediately.
4. **Expiration Settlement:**
   * At expiration, the contract checks Reflector oracle spot price.
   * If ITM, intrinsic payout is transferred to the buyer.
   * Any remaining collateral (including the 10% put buffer) is returned to the writer.

<h2 id="code-implementation">Code Reference</h2>

The client-side calculations and validation checks mirror `src/lib/collateral.ts` lines 8–10:

```typescript
// From src/lib/collateral.ts
export type OptionSide = "call" | "put";

export function collateralRequired(
  side: OptionSide,
  contracts: number,
  strike: number,
  spot: number
): number {
  return side === "call" ? contracts * spot : contracts * strike * 1.1;
}
```
